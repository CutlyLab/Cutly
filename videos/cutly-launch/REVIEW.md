# Cutly Launch Film — Review

## Creative calls

- Built as a 21-second industrial editorial launch film rather than a browser-product walkthrough.
- The narrative moves from verified artifact, to the browser constraint, to typed transactions, the native render pipeline, and evidence-bound output.
- The visual system uses bone paper, anodized black, and one cyan focus color. It deliberately avoids gradients, glow, glass, particles, generic dashboards, and decorative HUD styling.
- Music is an original local MusicGen score prompted for dry mechanical percussion, restrained sub bass, and a 112 BPM developer-tool launch rhythm. Three bundled effects mark verification, the cyan handoff, and the end-card landing.

## Timing and sound

- Detected score tempo: 112.4 BPM.
- Scene boundaries are locked to detected beats at 2.735, 4.895, 7.565, 11.315, 15.595, and 18.275 seconds.
- Final mix: -15.7 LUFS integrated, -11.8 LUFS short-term max, -1.0 dBTP, with no clipping.
- Delivery: 21.000 seconds, 1920×1080, 30 fps, H.264 video and 48 kHz stereo AAC audio.

## Verification

- `hyperframes check --samples 15`: pass; zero lint, runtime, layout, motion, contrast, craft, or audio findings.
- Review capture: 14 representative beats, all 6 scene seams, and phone-width legibility checked.
- Encoded MP4 frames were decoded and visually inspected after render.
- The cyan field at the reveal cut intentionally holds for roughly three frames before the first wordmark geometry lands; this is the main anticipation beat, not a missing frame.

## Alternatives considered

- A dark neon/glass treatment was rejected because it would read as a generic AI-tool promo and weaken Cutly's deterministic/native positioning.
- Continuous browser footage was rejected because the product differentiator is the typed document-to-native-render contract, not a webpage renderer.
- A denser sound-effects layer was rejected in favor of three causal accents, preserving the score's mechanical pulse.

## Weakest shot

The transaction scene carries the most information. It remains readable at full resolution and the review grid, but is intentionally the densest moment of the film. If a shorter social cut is created later, this is the first scene to simplify rather than speed up.
